Millimetres of edge error against the outline, positive meaning the model sits outside it. The upper waist and rim land within a third of a millimetre. All the error is in the lower waist and its neck, and it is there on purpose: forcing that waist to the upper one's 15.4mm wall costs about 2mm through that band. The substrate line crosses the lower waist in the photograph, which is the likely reason the outline reads it short — so the model says what is known about the object and disagrees with the picture exactly where the picture is least trustworthy.

What it is and is not

  • Exterior envelope only. A silhouette carries nothing about wall thickness, so none is modelled.
  • One anchor, everything else scaled. The base OD of 178.0 is the only confirmed diameter — a printed cradle fits it. Every other figure is that scale applied to the outline, good to a percent or two.
  • The R8 base roll is the one feature settled on the glass, by printing 45° cradle sections and comparing the curve directly. The photograph would prefer about 10mm; the glass wins.
  • Not in the spec document, deliberately. spec/vessel.md holds confirmed figures. These are photo-derived, and a second copy of them would drift from vessel/vessel.py, which is the definition.
  • A caliper across the body would still help. The raw phone photo reads the body about 4.5% wider than this one does.
